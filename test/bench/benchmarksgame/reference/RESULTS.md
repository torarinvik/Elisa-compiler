# Reference results: Rust and Zig at official sizes

Date 2026-10-13. Host: Apple M5, 10 cores, macOS arm64. Toolchains: rustc 1.99, zig 0.17.0.
Each program ran once (`RUNS=1 ./run_compare.sh <bench> official`) under `nice -n 10`
while the machine was in normal use. These are archival, exploratory observations;
they are not a compiler ranking or evidence of code-generation performance. The current
Benchmarksgame campaign is static-only, so do not rerun these cases for timing.

Wall time and user+sys are in seconds; RSS is peak resident set size in MB.
"MT" means multi-threaded and "ST" single-threaded. Where user+sys is much larger
than wall time, the program used several cores.

| bench | N | Rust prog | Rust wall | Rust usr+sys | Rust RSS | Zig prog | Zig wall | Zig usr+sys | Zig RSS | outputs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| binary-trees | 21 | #5 MT | 0.33 | 2.17 | 320 | 1.zig ST | 13.29 | 11.03 | 259 | equal, match site |
| fannkuch-redux | 12 | #4 MT | 2.39 | 17.80 | 2 | 2-m.zig MT | 2.77 | 22.11 | 2 | equal, match site |
| fasta | 25000000 | #9 MT | 0.78 | 2.03 | 5 | 1.zig ST | 0.70 | 0.67 | 2 | equal |
| k-nucleotide | fasta 25000000 | #7 MT | 0.43 | 2.14 | 134 | 1-m.zig MT | 0.80 | 6.12 | 251 | equal, match site |
| mandelbrot | 16000 | #4 MT | 0.28 | 2.56 | 33 | 1.zig ST | 1.97 | 1.96 | 33 | equal |
| n-body | 50000000 | #3 ST | 1.17 | 1.16 | 2 | 3.zig ST | 0.92 | 0.91 | 2 | equal, match site |
| pidigits | 10000 | #4 ST (GMP) | 0.40 | 0.40 | 4 | 1.zig ST (std.math.big) | 1.65 | 1.64 | 5 | equal |
| regex-redux | fasta 5000000 | #7 MT (PCRE2 JIT) | 0.45 | 2.18 | 150 | none | - | - | - | match site |
| reverse-complement | fasta 100000000 | #2 MT | 0.44 | 0.58 | 976 | none | - | - | - | - |
| spectral-norm | 5500 | #5 MT | 0.11 | 0.69 | 3 | 1.zig ST | 0.76 | 0.76 | 2 | equal, match site |

"Match site" means the official-size output equals the program output published on
the Benchmarks Game program page. At the small sizes, every built program
reproduces the site's `*-output.txt` byte for byte (see `expected/`).

Caveats:
- The Zig programs are not Benchmarks Game entries, because the site has never had
  Zig. They come from hanabi1224's Programming Language Benchmarks; see each
  `zig/*/PROVENANCE.md`. Several are simple single-threaded versions: binary-trees
  uses malloc per node, and pidigits uses std.math.big. These are not
  tuned peers of the Rust entries.
- Rust entries that use x86 intrinsics were rejected in favour of the next portable
  program: fannkuch #6, fasta #7, n-body #9 and #7, reverse-complement #1, and
  spectral-norm #6.
- Official fasta inputs are generated by `run_compare.sh` into `$DATA_DIR`, which
  defaults to `$TMPDIR/benchmarksgame-data`. They take about 50 MB, 250 MB and 1 GB.
